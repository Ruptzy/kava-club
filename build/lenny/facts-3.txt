# --- openings and their names
1.e4 and 1.d4 are by far the two most played first moves in chess.
Bobby Fischer called 1.e4 "best by test".
The Berlin Defence begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 Nf6.
The Marshall Attack is a pawn sacrifice for Black in the Ruy López: 8.c3 d5.
The Grünfeld begins 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 d5.
The Nimzo-Indian begins 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4.
The King's Indian begins 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6, with Black's bishop going to g7.
The Slav Defence begins 1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6.
The Semi-Slav combines ...c6 and ...e6 against the Queen's Gambit.
"Benoni" comes from Hebrew. It means "son of my sorrow", from the title of an 1825 book.
Bird's Opening, 1.f4, is named after the English player Henry Bird.
The Latvian Gambit begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 f5.
The Two Knights Defence begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6.
The Fried Liver Attack is the knight sacrifice on f7 after 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Nxd5 6.Nxf7.
The Philidor Defence begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 d6.
The Danish Gambit offers two pawns: 1.e4 e5 2.d4 exd4 3.c3.
The Halloween Gambit is a real opening. White gives up a knight on e5 on move four.
The Jerome Gambit gives up a bishop on f7 and then a knight on e5. It is considered unsound.
The Polish Opening, 1.b4, is also known as the Sokolsky.
The Alapin Variation of the Sicilian is 2.c3.
The Ponziani Opening is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.c3.
Petrov's Defence begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6.
Owen's Defence is 1.e4 b6.
The Modern Defence begins 1.e4 g6.
The Albin Countergambit is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e5.
The Trompowsky, 1.d4 Nf6 2.Bg5, is named after the Brazilian master Octávio Trompowsky.
The Colle System is named after the Belgian master Edgard Colle.
The Torre Attack is named after the Mexican grandmaster Carlos Torre.
The Bogo-Indian Defence is named after Efim Bogoljubov.
1.b3 is called the Larsen Opening, after Bent Larsen.
The Benko Gambit is also called the Volga Gambit.
The Elephant Gambit begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 d5.
The Traxler Counterattack answers 4.Ng5 in the Two Knights with 4...Bc5.
The Sveshnikov Sicilian is named after Evgeny Sveshnikov.
The Scheveningen Sicilian is named after a Dutch seaside town that hosted a tournament in 1923.
The Stonewall puts pawns on c3, d4, e3 and f4, or the mirror image for Black.
In the Hedgehog, Black's pawns sit on a6, b6, d6 and e6 and wait.
The Scotch Game begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4.
The Four Knights Game begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Nc3 Nf6.
The Queen's Indian Defence begins 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 b6.
The Tarrasch Defence is named after Siegbert Tarrasch, who said it was the only correct answer to the Queen's Gambit.
The Leningrad Dutch combines ...f5 with a kingside fianchetto.
The Rossolimo Sicilian, 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5, is named after Nicolas Rossolimo.
Nicolas Rossolimo ran a chess studio in New York and also drove a taxi.
The Wing Gambit against the Sicilian is 2.b4.
The Center Game begins 1.e4 e5 2.d4 exd4 3.Qxd4, bringing the queen out on move three.
The Accelerated Dragon plays ...g6 before ...d6 in the Sicilian.
The Najdorf starts with 5...a6, a pawn move that looks like it does nothing and does a lot.
The Exchange Variation of the French, 3.exd5 exd5, is famous for its symmetry.
The Winawer French begins 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Bb4.
The Advance Caro-Kann is 1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.e5.
The Panov Attack against the Caro-Kann often leads to an isolated queen's pawn.
The Ruy López Exchange Variation, 4.Bxc6, was a favourite weapon of Bobby Fischer.
The Smith-Morra Gambit is named after Pierre Morra of France and Ken Smith of Texas.
Ken Smith, of the Smith-Morra Gambit, was a professional poker player.
The Göring Gambit is the Scotch with 4.c3.
Paul Morphy played the Evans Gambit often.
The Dutch Defence was recommended by Elias Stein in 1789.
The Catalan combines d4 and c4 with a bishop on g2.
The English Opening begins 1.c4.
The Réti Opening begins 1.Nf3 and often waits to see what Black does.
The King's Indian Attack is White playing the King's Indian setup with an extra move.
The Pirc Defence begins 1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 g6.
The Scandinavian is also called the Centre Counter Defence.
The Nimzowitsch Defence begins 1.e4 Nc6.
The Englund Gambit has a well-known trap that mates White on move eight.
The Blackmar-Diemer Gambit begins 1.d4 d5 2.e4.
The Staunton Gambit against the Dutch is 1.d4 f5 2.e4.
ECO codes A00 to A99 are the flank openings. B is semi-open games, C is open games and French, D is closed games, E is Indian defences.

# --- rules and procedure
Under FIDE rules, your first illegal move in a standard game gives your opponent two extra minutes. The second one loses.
Under FIDE rules, if you deliberately touch an opponent's piece, you must capture it if you legally can.
Once you let go of a piece on a legal square, the move stands.
Under FIDE rules you must press the clock with the same hand you moved with.
Under FIDE rules an upside-down rook is a rook, not a queen.
If there is no spare queen when a pawn promotes, you stop the clock and ask the arbiter for one.
An arbiter is the official who runs a chess game or tournament and enforces the rules.
A draw by threefold repetition is claimed with the arbiter, not just announced to your opponent.
In descriptive notation, 1.e4 was written P-K4: pawn to king four.
In descriptive notation each player counts the ranks from their own side of the board.
Long algebraic notation writes both squares: 1.e2-e4.
PGN, Portable Game Notation, was created in 1993. Almost every chess program reads it.
FEN, Forsyth-Edwards Notation, describes a whole position in one line of text. David Forsyth devised it in 1883.
The International Correspondence Chess Federation was founded in 1951.
In Armageddon, White gets more time and Black wins the match with a draw.
The Buchholz tiebreak adds up the scores of everyone you played. Bruno Buchholz devised it.
The Sonneborn-Berger tiebreak rewards points scored against the strongest finishers.
In a double round robin, everyone plays everyone twice, once with each colour.
In a Scheveningen-system team match, each player plays every member of the other team.
A forfeit win counts as a win in the standings but is not rated.
Resigning is done by stopping the clock, saying so, or tipping your king over.
Offering a draw is done on your own move, after you play it and before you press the clock.
A draw offer stays open until your opponent accepts it, rejects it, or touches a piece.
Under FIDE rules, a mobile phone ringing in the playing hall can lose you the game.
In a rapid game under FIDE rules, you do not have to write your moves down.
The pawn is the only piece that captures differently from how it moves.
The king is the only piece that can never give check.

# --- endgames and theory
King and queen against a lone king can always be mated in ten moves or fewer.
King and rook against a lone king can always be mated in 16 moves or fewer.
Two bishops can always force mate against a lone king, in 19 moves or fewer.
Queen against rook is a theoretical win, and it is famously hard to win in practice.
Rook and bishop against rook is generally a draw, though some positions win.
Computers showed in the 1980s that two bishops usually beat a lone knight, overturning a century of theory.
A bishop and a rook pawn cannot win if the bishop does not control the promotion square and the defending king reaches the corner.
In king and pawn against king, the defending king usually draws by getting in front of the pawn with the opposition.
The Vančura position is a drawing method for rook against rook and a-pawn, named after Josef Vančura.
The Troitzky line marks how far back a pawn must be blocked for two knights to win against it.
Mutual zugzwang is a position where whichever side has to move loses.
A rook pawn is the hardest pawn to win with, because the defending king can hide in the corner.
Building a bridge, from the Lucena position, means using your rook to shield your king from checks.
Two connected passed pawns on the sixth rank usually beat a lone rook.
The "rule of the square" lets you check whether a king catches a passed pawn by drawing a square on the board.
Knight endings are said to be like pawn endings, because the knight cannot lose a move.
Queen against a pawn on the seventh rank wins, except that a rook pawn or a bishop pawn can often draw.

# --- words and phrases
"Kibitzer" comes from the German Kiebitz, a lapwing, a bird known for its noisy chatter.
A "woodpusher" is a weak player who moves pieces without much of a plan.
A "swindle" is escaping from a lost position by setting a trap.
"Coffeehouse chess" means tricky, risky play aimed at the opponent rather than the position.
"Hanging pawns" are two pawns side by side on the c and d files with no friendly pawns beside them.
The "minority attack" uses fewer pawns to attack more pawns, creating a weakness in the pawn chain.
The Carlsbad structure is named after tournaments held in Karlsbad.
The "hypermodern" school, led by Réti and Nimzowitsch, controlled the centre from a distance instead of occupying it.
The "romantic era" of the 1800s was full of gambits, sacrifices and attacks on the king.
"Blunder" probably comes from an Old Norse word for shutting your eyes. Very fitting.
In Spanish, check is "jaque" and checkmate is "jaque mate".
In French a draw is "nulle". In German it is "remis".
In German, castling is "Rochade".
"Pawn" comes from an old French word for foot soldier.
In French the bishop is "fou": the fool, or the jester.
In Russian the bishop is "slon", which means elephant.
The Russian word for the queen, "ferz", comes from the Persian word for a royal adviser.
In Spanish and Italian the rook is "torre", a tower.
"Elo" is pronounced "ee-low", like the first syllable of "eel".
"Zeitnot" is German, but "time trouble" in any language makes your hands shake the same way.
"Fish" is chess slang for an easy opponent. Do not use it at the club.
A "rook lift" is moving a rook up the board and across to the attack.
"Poisoned pawn" describes any pawn that looks free and is not.
A "quiet move" is one with no check, capture or direct threat, often the hardest kind to find.
A "zwischenzug" is also called an intermezzo.
A "pawn break" is the pawn move that opens the position for your pieces.
"Blockade" was one of Nimzowitsch's favourite words. He wrote a whole book on it.
"Bishop pair" means having both bishops when your opponent does not.
The "Dragon bishop" is Black's bishop on g7 in the Sicilian Dragon.
A "theoretical novelty" in an annotation is usually marked with N or TN.

# --- machines and online
Chess has been called the drosophila of artificial intelligence: the fruit fly the field experiments on.
In 2006 Vladimir Kramnik lost a six-game match to the program Deep Fritz.
AlphaZero beat Stockfish in a 100-game match in 2017 without losing a game.
TCEC, the Top Chess Engine Championship, has been won by Stockfish more times than by any other engine.
NNUE, the neural network inside modern Stockfish, was first invented for shogi programs.
Garry Kasparov invented "Advanced Chess", where humans play with computer help, in 1998.
A modern phone runs chess engines far stronger than Deep Blue was.
Engines measure their search in nodes per second. A node is one position looked at.
Opening books for engines are lists of prepared moves, just like a human's repertoire.
Chess.com and Lichess both rate players with versions of the Glicko system.
In daily chess each player has a day or more for every move.
Puzzle Rush, Puzzle Storm and Puzzle Streak are all timed puzzle modes on chess sites.
Lichess runs on donations. Every feature is free for everyone.
Chess.com reported more than 100 million members in 2022.
Online chess boomed during 2020, helped by lockdowns and "The Queen's Gambit".
Stockfish is named after dried cod, a Norwegian staple. Its first author, Tord Romstad, is Norwegian.
Komodo and Houdini were two of the strongest commercial engines of the 2010s.
"Centaur" is the name for a human and a computer playing together as a team.

# --- variants and relatives
Bughouse is played by teams of two on two boards. Pieces you capture go to your partner.
Crazyhouse is the one-board version of bughouse: you can drop captured pieces back on the board.
In atomic chess, a capture explodes every piece around it except pawns.
In three-check, you win by giving check three times.
In King of the Hill, you win by walking your king to one of the four central squares.
In Horde chess, White has 36 pawns and no king at all.
In antichess, captures are compulsory and you win by losing all your pieces.
In Racing Kings, nobody may give check, and the first king to reach the eighth rank wins.
In shogi, Japanese chess, captured pieces can be dropped back on the board as your own.
Xiangqi, Chinese chess, is played on the points of the lines, with a river across the middle.
Makruk, Thai chess, is one of the closest living relatives of the original Indian game.
In Kriegspiel you cannot see your opponent's pieces. An umpire tells you what is legal.
In xiangqi the two generals may not face each other on an open file.
In shogi a pawn may not be dropped to give immediate checkmate.
Chess960 was first played in a big event at Mainz in 2001.
In Chess960 the king always starts between the rooks, so castling still works.
The Freestyle Chess events of the 2020s use Chess960 starting positions.

# --- problems and studies
A helpmate is a problem where both sides cooperate to checkmate Black.
A selfmate is a problem where White forces Black to deliver mate.
Retrograde analysis works backward to find which moves must have led to a position.
The World Chess Solving Championship is a contest for solving composed problems against the clock.
A chess study is a composed position where White must win or draw, with no fixed number of moves.
A "key" in a chess problem is its first move. Good keys are hard to guess.
A "cook" is a second solution the composer did not intend. Composers hate them.
The Babson task, a set of matching underpromotions, was thought impossible until Leonid Yarosh composed it in 1983.

# --- odd corners
With the 50-move rule, the longest possible chess game is 5,949 moves.
The knight is the only piece that can jump over others.
A pawn that reaches the far side of the board is promoted on the same move. It never waits a turn.
FIDE recommends a tournament king about 9.5 centimetres tall.
FIDE recommends board squares between 5 and 6 centimetres wide.
The Staunton knight is said to be modelled on a horse's head from the Elgin Marbles.
Chess sets made of wood are usually boxwood for the light pieces and ebony or rosewood for the dark ones.
Weighted pieces have lead or steel in the base so they stand firm and feel solid.
In 1965 Bobby Fischer played the Capablanca Memorial in Havana by teletype, from the Marshall Chess Club in New York.
The first chess game played by telegraph was between Washington and Baltimore in 1844.
A board's light square in the bottom right is the rule. "Light on right" is how players remember it.
The 1924 Paris Olympics included an unofficial chess olympiad.
Chessboxing alternates rounds of chess with rounds of boxing. You can win by knockout or by checkmate.
The International Olympic Committee recognised chess as a sport in 1999.
FIDE has more than 190 member federations.
Chess clocks were sand-glasses before they were mechanical.
Digital clocks with increment became standard in top events in the 1990s.
The DGT digital clock and electronic board are why broadcasts can show moves live.
Sound-deadening felt under the pieces is called baize.

The Woodpecker Method is named after one of its authors, Hans Tikkanen. "Tikka" is Finnish for woodpecker.
The Taimanov Variation is named after Mark Taimanov, a grandmaster who was also a concert pianist.
In a Swiss tournament, players on the same score are paired against each other each round.
The four central squares are d4, e4, d5 and e5. Every opening fights over them.
Round-robin, Swiss and knockout are the three classic tournament formats.
