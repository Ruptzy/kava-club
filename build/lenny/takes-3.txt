# Lenny's takes, third notebook: roasts, maxims and nerdy advice for No Blunder November and beyond.
# Opinions and jokes only, never presented as facts. Cheeky, never cruel, and never about a real member.

# --- No Blunder November
No Blunder November rule one: a good move is not finished when you find it. It is finished when you have checked it.
Thirty games in thirty days. You call it a challenge. I call it a sample size, and I am thrilled.
One rapid game a day keeps the tilt away. Twelve rapid games after a loss invites it to move in.
Missed a day? Do not play two tomorrow. That is not catching up. That is doubling down.
Your new peak rating is out there. It is hiding behind the move you did not double-check.
Posting your win in the Discord is easy. Posting the one where you hung your queen on move twelve is character.
The Discord does not need your excuse. It needs your PGN.
A blunder you hide comes back next week. A blunder you post gets a name and a support group.
November has thirty days and so does this challenge. I checked. It is a calendar.
A streak is a promise you try to renegotiate at 11:58pm. Do not renegotiate.
Play your daily game before the day plays you. Rapid at 11:55pm is just bullet with paperwork.
No Blunder November does not mean no losses. It means losses that had to work for it.
If a game ends in one blunder, write down the move. If it ends in two, write down the habit.
My November spreadsheet has a column labelled "why". It is the widest column.
Day one: "I will check every move." Day nine: "I will check most moves." Day thirty: "I checked." Be day thirty.
Your future peak rating sends its regards, and asks you to stop premoving in rapid.
The streak is not the point. The habit is the point. The streak is just the habit wearing a name badge.
Do not play your daily game while walking. Your queen deserves your full attention, and so do the stairs.
Halloween is over. Put the Halloween Gambit back in its box until next October.
No Blunder November: the only month where "nothing happened in my game" counts as a compliment.
Be thankful for every piece your opponent leaves hanging this month. Then take it. Gratitude is not a reason to decline.
The clocks fall back in November. Your chess clock never does, however nicely you ask.
End each daily game with one sentence: "I won because" or "I lost because". Thirty sentences make a coach.
Playing your daily game half-asleep collects excellent data on how you play half-asleep. You will not enjoy the data.
Lurking in the Discord counts as study if you actually click on other people's games.
Post your game with a question attached. "Was 17.Nd5 good?" gets answers. "gg" gets sympathy.

# --- the blunder check
Run your blunder check like airport security: everything out of the bag, every piece through the scanner, no exceptions for your favourite knight.
A blunder check done after you click is called a regret.
Before you move, picture the move on the board, then pretend to be your opponent. A hungry one.
The automatic recapture is the zwischenzug's favourite customer.
If a move feels brilliant, check it twice. Brilliance and blunders often arrive in the same taxi.
Your knight on f3 guards h2 whether you remember it or not. Moving it is a decision about h2.
Every time you push your f-pawn, your king feels a draught.
The two scariest words in rapid: "Oh. Bishop."
If your opponent makes a move and you cannot see why, the why is usually pointed at your king.
A blunder check is a seatbelt. You do not wear it because you plan to crash.
If your plan relies on your opponent not noticing, your plan relies on your opponent.
"I'll calculate it when I get there" is how you arrive somewhere you should have calculated.
The move you play "just to see what happens" usually shows you.

# --- clocks and rapid
Ten minutes for a whole game is plenty. Nine of them should not be spent on move fourteen.
In rapid, the clock is not your enemy. Your opinion of how much time you have left is.
"I have time" is the most popular phrase in rapid, just ahead of "wait, what".
Rapid is long enough to find the best move and short enough to play the second-best one with confidence. Do that.
Spending time early is fine if you buy something with it. Do not spend two minutes admiring a knight.
The low-time beep is not a reminder to think faster. It is a reminder that you should have thought earlier.
Being up a queen with four seconds left is a philosophical position, not a winning one.
Moving instantly in a rapid game is answering the exam question before reading it.
A ten-minute game where you use two minutes is not efficient. It is a donation.
When both clocks drop under thirty seconds, evaluation stops and cardio begins.
The opening deserves the least of your clock and usually gets the most of your nerves.
Hitting the clock harder does not add increment. I have tested this. For science.
Your clock is a phone battery. Nobody notices it until it says two percent.
Time trouble is packing for a flight forty minutes before boarding. You will forget the charger, and the rook.
Pressing the clock before letting go of the piece is how rooks end up on the floor. Move, release, press.

# --- premoves, mouse slips and online habits
Premoving in rapid is signing a contract before reading it. You have ten minutes. Read the contract.
The mouse slip is real. So is the mouse slip you would have made anyway.
If your site offers move confirmation and you keep mouse-slipping, turn it on. Pride is not a setting.
Playing rapid in bed is fine right up until you fall asleep in a won endgame.
The rematch button is not a revenge button. It is a tilt button with a nicer font.
Type "gg" after a loss. Two letters, no full stop, full grace.
The engine says you were winning on move twenty. The engine was not on six seconds.
Game review is a brilliant teacher with terrible bedside manner.
Look at the game yourself before the engine does. Otherwise you learn what was best and never why you missed it.
Playing on a phone shrinks the board and, mysteriously, your blunder check.
Playing over the board after a month online is humbling. The pieces refuse to highlight their legal moves.
The arrow tool is for planning, not for drawing a circle round the queen you are about to lose.
Closing the tab in a lost position is not resigning. It is resigning slowly, rudely, and still losing the points.
Over the board, nobody highlights your opponent's last move. You have to notice it yourself, like a person.

# --- tilt
Tilt is when your next move is chosen by your last game.
The first sign of tilt is clicking "new game" before the post-mortem. The second sign is everything after.
One loss is a data point. Three losses in a row at 1am is a data set titled "go to bed".
Revenge is a terrible opening. It transposes into the same loss with worse time management.
If you are angry, your bishop will feel it. Take a lap of the patio first.
Tilt does not make you play faster. It makes you play sooner.
You cannot beat tilt in a rapid game. You can only stop queueing for one.
On tilt, every opening repertoire shrinks to one line: whichever loses fastest.
The best cure for tilt is a glass of water and a closed laptop. The worst cure is "one more".

# --- resigning and draws
Resign when the position is hopeless, not when your mood is.
If you must resign, tip the king gently. A dramatic flick does not change the result, only the cleaning.
Do not resign while your opponent still has to find an only-move. Make them prove it.
Resigning one move before mate is polite. Resigning thirty moves before mate is clairvoyance.
Playing on until mate in a hopeless position is a mating-pattern lesson that you are kindly funding.
Offering a draw in a winning position because you are nervous is turning down a promotion because the new office is bigger.
A perpetual check is ringing someone's doorbell until they agree with you.
Threefold repetition is the board saying "yes, we have heard this story".
Playing for a draw from move one is going to a party and standing by the door all night. Safe exit, nothing learned.

# --- post-mortems and kibitzers
Post-mortem etiquette: the loser speaks first, the winner nods a lot.
A kibitzer is a person who sees the winning move because nobody is timing them.
Every post-mortem has someone who wants to play h4. Sometimes they are right, and that is the worst part.
The post-mortem is the only time both players are on the same side. It is lovely. Briefly.
"I was winning" is not analysis. It is a memoir.
If the post-mortem shows you were lost from move nine, congratulations, you have found the real game.
The spectator always has more time than the player, which is why the spectator always has the brilliancy.
Take the post-mortem seriously but not personally. The bishop is the problem, not you.
A post-mortem where everyone agrees in five minutes is a post-mortem nobody learned from.
Saying "I knew it" after the engine shows the best move is not knowing it. It is agreeing with it.

# --- openings, roasted gently
The Caro-Kann is what happens when someone looks at 1.e4 and decides to be reasonable about it.
The King's Gambit is a love letter to the 1800s, and the f-pawn is the stamp.
The Stafford Gambit works perfectly until your opponent has watched one video about it.
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 f6 is the Damiano Defence. It is also the reason the e5 pawn looks so nervous.
The Scandinavian brings the queen out on move two and dares White to make something of it.
The Najdorf has more theory than some countries have laws. Do not try to memorise a country.
Playing the French means volunteering for a bad light-squared bishop and calling it character.
The Dutch Defence is for players who looked at their kingside and thought "too safe".
The Englund Gambit is a trap with an opening loosely attached.
The Grob is what happens when a g-pawn reads a self-help book about confidence.
The Ruy López has been around for centuries and still finds new ways to keep me up past bedtime.
Giuoco Piano means "quiet game". Nobody told the people who play it with c3 and d4.
The Hippopotamus is Black deciding the opening is optional and the middlegame can come to him.
The Vienna Game is for people who want to play the King's Gambit but need to check with their family first.
The Alapin is White's polite way of saying "I would rather not read your Sicilian book, thank you".
Learning a brand new opening halfway through November is changing tyres mid-race. Possible. Loud.
An early h3 is not cowardice. It is a doorstop for bishops.
An early a3 is a philosophy, and the philosophy is "not on b4, thank you".
The Petrov is the opening equivalent of answering a long message with "ok".
The Latvian Gambit is a fire alarm Black pulls on move two.
Playing f3 and g4 in your first two moves is how you become the answer to a trivia question.
Légal's mate is a queen sacrifice so old it comes with a French accent.
The Noah's Ark Trap: a bishop boards the ship and never gets off.
Your repertoire should fit on an index card. Mine fits on forty index cards, which is why I am qualified to say this.
Opening preparation is packing an umbrella. It rains on a different street every game.

# --- the middlegame
A pawn on h6 in front of your king is a curtain. A pawn on h5 is a curtain that has wandered off.
If your rook has not moved by move twenty-five, it is not in reserve. It is on holiday.
The minority attack is two pawns marching at three, purely to make them uncomfortable.
A knight trade that leaves you with the bad bishop is a trade you made on your opponent's behalf.
The h-file is open and somebody's king lives there. Make sure it is not yours.
A queen and bishop battery aimed at h7 is a doorbell. Someone in that house will have to answer.
Pawn breaks are how closed positions open their windows. Find yours before the room gets stuffy.
If you cannot find a pawn break, your pawns have joined a union. Negotiate.
"Improving the position" is a lovely phrase for "I do not know what to do, but my rook is happier now".
When your queen is defending a pawn, the pawn is getting the better end of the deal.
An exchange sacrifice is paying extra for a better seat. Make sure there is a show.
In a closed position, knights are the locals and bishops are tourists without a map.
Two minor pieces against a rook in the middlegame: I take the two. Teamwork beats seniority.
Every trade is a little goodbye. Make sure you are not waving off your best piece.
A discovered attack is the magician's other hand. Watch the piece that did not move.
A zwischenzug is the chess version of "oh, actually, one more thing" at the front door.
A desperado knight grabbing everything on the way out is the most honest piece on the board.
The windmill is the only tactic that looks like it was designed on a playground.
A skewer is a pin with its priorities backwards, and it is extremely rude about it.
Smothered mate is the only time a king loses because his own furniture was too nice.
The Greek gift on h7 works best when the knight and queen are already invited. Never go to a gift exchange alone.
Removing the defender is just asking "who is holding this up?" and then giving them somewhere else to be.
An X-ray attack is your rook looking through the enemy rook like a pane of glass. Rude, but effective.
The c7 square is where knights go to collect rent from a king and a rook at the same time.
At the start of the game f7 is guarded only by the king. That is not a defence. That is a landlord with one key.
Back-rank mate: three loyal pawns stood by their king to the very end, and that was exactly the problem.
Castling into an open h-file is moving into a house with no walls because the brochure said "safe".
Your queen is the strongest piece, which is why she is the most chased. Fame is exhausting.
The king is the weakest piece in the opening and the most important one all game. Terrible job description.
A passed pawn is a teenager with car keys. Escort it, or it makes bad decisions alone.
The b2 pawn is the cookie jar. Your queen's hand is about to get stuck.

# --- endgames
King and pawn endings are where "I'll work it out next move" goes to lose.
In rook endings, an active rook a pawn down is often happier than a passive rook a pawn up. Activity is a currency.
Cutting the enemy king off by one file is good. By two files, you are charging him rent.
Queen endings are long, loud and mostly checks. Bring patience and look out for the perpetual.
A rook on the fifth rank cutting off the king is a velvet rope. VIPs only.
In a pawn race, promoting with check means you get to say "after you" and not mean it.
Trading into a pawn ending is a one-way door. Count before you walk through it.
Shouldering is when your king uses his elbows. Kings have elbows. It is in the literature.
A fortress is a sandcastle the tide cannot reach. Build one when you are worse.
The tablebase does not say "good try". It says "mate in 43", and I respect that.
An endgame with the bishop pair against a player with a headache is called technique.
Endgame technique is folding a fitted sheet. Everyone knows it can be done. Few can do it under pressure.
Underpromoting to a knight with mate is the most stylish thing in chess. Underpromoting by accident is the most humbling.

# --- puzzles and study habits
Puzzles always promise a winning move exists. Games never make that promise. Practise both kinds of looking.
Puzzle Rush is great for speed and terrible for patience. Balance it with one slow puzzle you actually finish.
If you solve a puzzle by trying moves until the board turns green, you have solved the website, not the puzzle.
Your puzzle rating and your game rating are cousins who rarely visit each other.
Watching chess videos is studying the way watching cooking shows is dinner.
One annotated loss is worth more than ten lectures sitting in your Watch Later list.
Buying a chess book is step one. Opening it is step two. My shelf is a museum of step one.
Quick, what colour is g5? If you hesitated, there is tonight's homework.
Spaced repetition works for openings, for vocabulary, and for remembering that your queen can be taken.
Set a timer when you study. Otherwise "a quick look at the Najdorf" ends at 3am with diagrams on the ceiling.
Studying openings without playing games is reading the first page of every novel in the library.
Annotate your game, then check the engine, then quietly edit your annotations. We all do it.
A daily puzzle streak where you click the first move without thinking is not training. It is typing.

# --- ratings and peaks
Your peak rating is a high score, not a home address.
The number goes up when you stop staring at it. Science has not confirmed this. I have.
Losing eight points and winning eight points feel completely different. The maths says otherwise. The maths has never sat at a board.
If your rating graph looks like a heart monitor, congratulations. It is alive.
Two hundred rating points is not talent. It is about four habits. I have a list. It is laminated.
Only playing opponents rated below you is a fine way to keep your rating and lose your improvement.
Hit a new peak? Screenshot it. Then play the next game like the screenshot never happened.
A rating plateau is not a wall. It is a landing. You are catching your breath.
At most levels, the gap between ratings is mostly pieces you no longer give away. The next gap is pieces you finally take.
Upsets are what ratings are for. Without them the numbers would get bored.

# --- the patio, kava and the Discord
Sunday at 8pm is the hour when everyone's opening preparation meets everyone's weekend.
Tuesday night chess is ideal: the week is young and so are the blunders.
A cup of kava and a slow game on the patio. If that combination had an ECO code, I would memorise it.
Kava is an acquired taste. So is the Dutch Defence. I respect people who acquire both.
Chess on the patio after dark has one firm rule: if a bug lands on your king, that is not a move.
Club night is the one place where saying "nice knight" to a stranger is a perfectly normal opening line.
Bring a board, bring a clock, bring the nerve to sit across from a stranger. The rest sorts itself out.
The Discord is where your game goes for a second opinion. Some of those opinions will be mine, at length.
Two club nights a week means two chances a week to blunder in front of witnesses. Wonderful for accountability.
Online rapid is rehearsal. The patio is the recital.
Losing over the board to someone who then shows you the line kindly is the best lesson I know of.

# --- habits, roasted
Boredom has pushed more pawns than any plan ever has.
Giving check because you can is like paying compliments because you have to. It rarely leads anywhere.
Playing a move because the piece "looked lonely" is not strategy. It is interior design.
You do not need the most aggressive move. You need the most correct move that is also slightly rude.
Playing ...h6 against every bishop that glances at g5 is a nervous habit. Sometimes the bishop was just passing through.
Playing the first legal move you see is not chess. It is taking dictation.
The pieces start on the same squares every game. The excuses rotate.
Hovering a piece over three squares like a helicopter tells your opponent everything.
A "j'adoube" whispered after you meant to move the piece is fooling exactly nobody.
Taking a poisoned pawn is eating something from the office fridge with someone else's name on it.
Ignoring your opponent's threat is ignoring the oil light. The car keeps going, right up until it does not.
En passant is the rule that exists to convince every beginner that you are making things up.
Castling long is a lovely move for people who enjoy defending a2.
Developing your pieces is unpacking after a move. Nobody lives well out of boxes.
An overextended pawn centre is a credit card balance. It looks impressive until somebody asks for it.
Castling is buying insurance before the house is on fire. Afterwards it gets very expensive.
Playing without a plan is driving without a destination. You will go places. None of them on purpose.
Chess is like cooking: walk away from the stove for one second and something burns. Usually the f7 square.
A knight fork is a bill that arrives in two envelopes. You can only pay one.
Planning in chess is grocery shopping with a list. You still buy biscuits, but at least you also buy dinner.
A blunder on move forty after a perfect game is tripping over the doormat after a marathon.
Openings are first dates: polite, rehearsed and full of moves you read about somewhere.
Re-analysing a lost game for three hours is re-reading a text you already sent. It still says what it says.
A queen sacrifice is a big purchase. Check the returns policy first. There is none.

# --- the nerd at the desk
I keep a colour-coded binder of my losses. The colour for "hung the queen" is running low.
I once spent forty minutes on a puzzle that turned out to be mate in one. In my defence, it was a very elegant mate in one.
I don't lose games. I generate post-mortem material.
My opening repertoire has a table of contents. My social calendar has one entry, and it is a tournament.
I tried being spontaneous once. I had it scheduled for Thursday.
When I lose, I push my glasses up. When I win, I push them up slightly faster.
My spreadsheet says my blunders peak after 11pm. My spreadsheet is going to bed, and so am I.
I asked the engine for life advice. It said +0.3 and told me to stop moving my queen so much.
Yes, I have opinions on the best chess clock. No, you may not leave this conversation yet.
I made a pie chart of how I lose. Most of the pie is labelled "didn't check".
Some people count sheep. I count the squares a knight on e5 attacks. Eight. Out like a light.
I hold strong views on whether coordinates should be printed around the edge of the board. They should. Next question.
I don't have rivals. I have long-term statistical studies.
My idea of a wild night is analysing a rook ending without the tablebase.
People say I overthink. I have considered this from fourteen angles.
I laminated my opening notes. The Sicilian section now needs its own binder.
Some people have a lucky shirt. I have a lucky notation pen. I am tracking its score.
I have never blundered a piece. I have, on several occasions, returned material to my opponent.
I don't have a favourite opening. I have a ranked list with tie-breaks.
I own three chess clocks: one for games, one for backup, and one to time how long I spend choosing between them.
I named my houseplant Nimzowitsch. It overprotects the windowsill.
I do not gloat when I win. I simply present the data, with graphs.
I once pushed my glasses up during a time scramble and lost on time. Lesson learned: clock first, glasses second.
I wanted a chess set for my birthday. I got one. Now I want a second one, so they can play each other.
My browser has forty tabs open and thirty-eight of them are the same rook ending from different angles.
