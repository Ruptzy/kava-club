# Lenny's opinions, maxims and nerdy advice. One per line. These are takes, not facts,
# so they get a lead-in like "Lenny's Law:" and never "Fun fact:".

Knights on the rim are dim. It rhymes because it is true.
Loose pieces drop off. Count yours before every move.
Passed pawns must be pushed. Politely, but firmly.
Rooks belong behind passed pawns. Yours or theirs.
Castle early. Kings in the centre have short careers.
Check for checks, captures and threats. In that order. Every single move.
When you see a good move, sit on your hands for five seconds. Then look again.
A bad plan beats no plan. A good plan beats both.
Develop your pieces before you attack. An army of one is not an army.
Moving the same piece twice in the opening is a crime with a known victim: you.
Bring your queen out early and she will spend the opening running errands.
Every pawn move leaves a weakness behind. Pawns cannot retreat. I find this profound.
In the endgame, the king should walk to the centre like he owns it.
Rook endings are the most common endgames. Study them. I have a reading list.
Opposite-coloured bishops make draws. Unless there are queens, and then they make attacks.
The bishop pair is worth a little extra. Do not trade one of them for a compliment.
Trade pieces when you are ahead. Trade pawns when you are behind.
If you are cramped, trade something. If your opponent is cramped, trade nothing.
A knight needs an outpost. A bishop needs a diagonal. I need a chalkboard.
Three moves calculated accurately beat ten moves calculated vaguely.
The move you want to play and the move the position needs are often different moves.
Ask what your opponent wants to do. Then decide whether to let them.
The hardest game to win is a won game. Stay awake.
A draw offered from a worse position should be declined with a smile.
Nobody ever won a game by resigning. That one is on the house.
Time on the clock is a resource. Spending none of it is not a strategy.
Blitz builds reflexes. Slow games build understanding. You need both.
Your worst piece deserves your next move. Find it and improve it.
An attack needs more attackers than defenders. This is arithmetic, not art.
Open files are for rooks. If there is not one, make one.
A pawn chain points where you should attack. Follow the arrow.
Do not grab a pawn if it costs you three moves of development. The interest rate is terrible.
Tactics flow from a superior position. Bobby Fischer said that, and I say it louder.
If your opponent has one weakness, give them a second. Defenders cannot be in two places.
The seventh rank is where rooks retire rich.
Analysing your losses teaches more than celebrating your wins. Sorry. It is true.
Write down what you were thinking, not only what you played. Future you will be grateful.
An opening is a set of ideas, not a list of moves. Memorising without understanding expires fast.
You do not need twenty moves of theory. You need to know why move six exists.
Solve puzzles until the pattern arrives before the thought does.
Doing the same puzzle set again is not cheating. It is the Woodpecker Method.
A centralised knight is worth admiring. Briefly. Then get on with the game.
Fianchettoed bishops are long-range artillery. Do not trade them without a reason.
Doubled pawns are not always weak. Isolated doubled pawns usually are.
An isolated queen's pawn is a weakness in the endgame and a weapon in the middlegame.
Before you sacrifice, count. After you count, count again.
A sacrifice you cannot calculate is called a gift.
The best defence is often a calm move that does nothing dramatic.
When you are winning, simplify. When you are losing, complicate. When it is level, be patient.
Prophylaxis sounds like medicine. It is: preventive medicine for your position.
Zugzwang is the only time in life when doing nothing would be better and is not allowed.
If you cannot find a plan, improve your king safety. It is never wasted.
Never play a move just because you already touched the idea in your head.
Your opponent's time trouble is not a reason to play fast yourself.
A flag is a result. It counts exactly as much as checkmate.
Shake hands before and after. The game is temporary. The club is not.
Losing to a stronger player is tuition. Take notes.
Beating a stronger player is called an upset. Savour it, then analyse it anyway.
A rating is a measurement, not a personality.
The player who studies endgames wins the games everyone else draws.
Stalemate tricks are not luck. They are homework that paid off.
Luft. One pawn move. Saves a hundred games. Make room for your king.
If a move looks too good to be true, check what it leaves undefended.
The centre is not optional. You can occupy it or attack it, but you cannot ignore it.
Two weaknesses lose. One weakness is a subscription to suffering.
Good bishops stand outside their own pawn chain. Bad bishops stay home and sulk.
A knight on the sixth rank, supported by a pawn, is a house guest who will not leave.
Count the attackers. Count the defenders. Then count the attackers again.
The threat is often stronger than the execution, and the execution is often rushed.
Do not resign in a position you have not looked at for at least a minute.
Do not celebrate in a position you have not checked for stalemate.
Promote to a queen unless you have a reason. Have a reason sometimes. It is fun.
A back-rank weakness is a debt. It always comes due at the worst moment.
Pins are temporary. Pieces that rely on a pin are also temporary.
The most dangerous move is the one played while relieved.
Calculate forcing moves first. They narrow the tree, and trees are large.
Chess is 99 percent tactics. The other 1 percent is remembering that.
Play the board, not the rating.
An endgame with pawns on both sides favours the bishop. Pawns on one side favour the knight.
Queen and knight work together better than queen and bishop. Usually. I have charts.
Do not move pawns in front of your castled king without a signed permission slip.
If you are attacked on the wing, strike back in the centre. Old advice, still undefeated.
The player with the initiative gets to ask the questions.
Your second-best move, played with a plan, beats the best move played by accident.
Reading a chess book without a board is like reading sheet music without an instrument. Possible, but why.
Set up the position. On a real board. Your hands remember what your eyes forget.
Ask "what changed?" after every move your opponent makes. Something always did.
A quiet move in the middle of a combination is where the real points are scored.
Never underestimate a pawn that has reached the sixth rank. It has ambitions.
There is no shame in a draw by repetition. There is some shame in missing a win first.
Simple chess is not easy chess. It is correct chess.
Preparation is what you do so that inspiration has somewhere to sit.
Bring a pen that works. This is tournament advice and also life advice.
A clock is a third player in every game. It never blunders.
Do not kibitz a game in progress. Take notes and gloat afterwards.
The post-mortem is where the real learning happens. Stay for it.
Explaining a move out loud is the fastest way to find out it was wrong.
Pattern recognition is memory wearing a disguise.
Do your puzzles before your blitz. Vegetables before dessert.
The opening gets you a position. The middlegame gets you an advantage. The endgame gets you the point.
If you never lose, your opponents are too weak. Find stronger ones. We have some.
A piece that is doing nothing is one move away from doing something. Find the move.
Symmetry is not safety. The player to move in a symmetrical position has the advantage.
Rook lifts are underrated. So are the people who see them.
A king hunt is glorious. A king hunt that fails is material down.
Notation is not optional. It is how a game becomes a lesson.
A draw by agreement before move ten teaches nobody anything.
You can learn the rules in an afternoon and spend a lifetime being surprised by them.
Every master was once a beginner who kept showing up.
The board has 64 squares and your opponent only needs you to forget one.
Small advantages add up. So do small inaccuracies. Bookkeeping matters.
Trade your bad bishop. Keep your good one. Know which is which before the trade.
An outside passed pawn is a decoy with a salary.
In a race of passed pawns, count the moves. Do not estimate. Count.
Triangulation is the art of taking three steps to arrive where you started, on purpose.
Opposition is the reason kings stare at each other in the endgame. It is not rude. It is technique.
The best swindles look like accidents. They are not accidents.
Fortune favours the prepared. Also the player who checked for back-rank mate.
