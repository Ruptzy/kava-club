# --- numbers and geometry
There are exactly 20 legal first moves for White.
After one move each there are 400 possible positions.
After two moves each there are 197,281 possible games.
Claude Shannon estimated about 10^120 possible chess games. The observable universe has roughly 10^80 atoms.
A chessboard has 64 squares, but if you count squares of every size there are 204.
There are 1,296 rectangles of every size on a chessboard.
A rook on an empty board controls exactly 14 squares, wherever it stands.
A queen in the centre of an empty board controls 27 squares.
A queen in the corner of an empty board still controls 21 squares.
A bishop controls 13 squares from the centre of an empty board and only 7 from the corner.
A knight controls 8 squares from the centre and just 2 from the corner.
A king controls 8 squares in the middle of the board, 5 on the edge and 3 in the corner.
There are 92 ways to place eight queens so that none attacks another. Only 12 of them are truly different.
The eight queens puzzle was first posed in 1848.
The most legal moves known in any reachable position is 218.
The fastest possible checkmate takes two moves. It is called Fool's Mate, and White has to cooperate.
Scholar's Mate takes four moves.
A knight needs six moves to travel from one corner to the opposite corner.
A king needs seven moves to walk from one edge of the board to the other.
A pawn can promote in as few as five moves from its starting square.
One side can legally have nine queens on the board at once.
Each side starts with 16 pieces, and half of them are pawns.
In the starting position, the knights are the only pieces that can move before a pawn does.
The standard piece values are pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9. The king has no price.
A knight always lands on the opposite colour from the square it left.
A knight's tour visits all 64 squares exactly once. Euler studied it in the 1700s.
Counting direction, there are 26,534,728,821,064 closed knight's tours on a chessboard.
Chess960 has exactly 960 starting positions.
The number of legal chess positions is estimated at about 4.8 x 10^44.
In the wheat and chessboard story, doubling one grain across 64 squares gives more than 18 quintillion grains.
The longest forced mate found in seven-piece endgame tablebases takes 549 moves.
King, bishop and knight can always force mate against a lone king, in at most 33 moves.
Two knights cannot force checkmate against a lone king.
A light-squared bishop can only ever reach 32 of the 64 squares.
Every position with seven pieces or fewer has been solved by endgame tablebases.
A bishop never leaves the colour it starts on.
The square a1 is always a dark square.
The board has 32 light squares and 32 dark squares.
A knight in the centre can reach any square on the board in four moves or fewer.
The two longest diagonals on the board are eight squares long. There is one of each colour.
A rook can reach any square on an empty board in at most two moves.
A queen can reach any square on an empty board in at most two moves.
A bishop can reach any square of its own colour on an empty board in at most two moves.
There are 16 pawns at the start of a game, and none of them can ever move backward.
There are eight files and eight ranks. Files are lettered a to h and ranks are numbered 1 to 8.

# --- rules
Castling is the only move where two of your own pieces move at once.
You cannot castle out of check, through check, or into check.
You can castle while your rook is under attack. Only the king's squares matter.
When castling queenside, the rook may cross an attacked square. The king may not.
Castling is written O-O on the kingside and O-O-O on the queenside.
Under FIDE rules you must touch the king first when you castle.
You may not castle with a king or a rook that has already moved, even if it moved back.
En passant is only legal on the very next move.
En passant is the only capture where the capturing piece does not land on the square of the piece it takes.
En passant is French for "in passing".
The pawn's two-square first move is the reason en passant exists.
A promoting pawn can become a queen, rook, bishop or knight. Never a king.
Promoting to anything other than a queen is called underpromotion.
You may promote to a second queen while your first one is still on the board.
Stalemate is a draw, not a win.
A king is never actually captured. The game ends one move earlier.
Two kings can never stand on adjacent squares.
A double check can only be answered by moving the king.
A pinned piece can still give check.
You can deliver checkmate by castling.
You can deliver checkmate with an en passant capture.
Under touch-move, if you deliberately touch a piece you must move it if it has a legal move.
"J'adoube" means "I adjust". You say it before straightening a piece.
The board is set up with a light square in each player's right-hand corner.
The queen starts on her own colour: the white queen on a light square, the black queen on a dark one.
White moves first. That only became the standard convention in the late 1800s.
The 50-move rule resets with every capture and every pawn move.
Since 2014 a game is automatically drawn after 75 moves without a capture or pawn move, even if nobody claims it.
Fivefold repetition ends a game automatically. Threefold repetition has to be claimed.
A position where no sequence of legal moves could ever give mate is an immediate draw.
King against king is a draw the moment it appears.
King and bishop against a lone king is a draw.
King and knight against a lone king is a draw.
If your flag falls but your opponent has no possible way to mate you, the game is a draw.
Under FIDE rules you may not write a move down before you play it.
In algebraic notation the knight is N, because K was already taken by the king.
A plus sign means check. A hash sign means checkmate.
In annotations, "!!" is a brilliant move, "??" a blunder, "!?" interesting and "?!" dubious.
FIDE made algebraic notation the standard in 1981.
In Spanish notation the pieces are R, D, T, A and C: rey, dama, torre, alfil, caballo.
In German notation the knight is S, for Springer, and the bishop is L, for Läufer.
Under FIDE rules, rapid chess means more than 10 and less than 60 minutes per player.
Under FIDE rules, blitz means 10 minutes or less per player.
An increment adds time to your clock after every move.
Bobby Fischer filed a patent for a chess clock with increments in 1988.
A time control written G/30 means each player has 30 minutes for the whole game.
Threefold repetition does not need the positions to occur one after another.
For a repetition claim, the same player must be to move and the same moves must be possible each time.

# --- history
Chess descends from chaturanga, a game played in India around the sixth century.
The Persian form of chess was called shatranj.
"Checkmate" comes from the Persian "shah mat", roughly "the king is helpless".
"Rook" comes from the Persian "rukh", a chariot.
The bishop began as an elephant. In Spanish it is still "alfil", from the Arabic for elephant.
The queen began as a counsellor that moved only one square diagonally.
The modern queen appeared in Europe in the late 1400s. Players called the new game "mad queen chess".
The oldest recorded game with modern rules comes from a Valencian poem, "Scachs d'amor", from around 1475.
Luis Ramírez de Lucena published the oldest surviving printed chess book in 1497.
Ruy López, a Spanish priest, published his chess book in 1561. The opening still carries his name.
The Staunton chess pattern dates from 1849. It was designed by Nathaniel Cooke and endorsed by Howard Staunton.
The first international chess tournament was London 1851. Adolf Anderssen won it.
The Immortal Game was played in London in 1851. Anderssen gave up a bishop, both rooks and his queen, then mated.
The Evergreen Game was played in 1852, and Anderssen won that one too.
Paul Morphy played the Opera Game in Paris in 1858, against a duke and a count playing together.
Paul Morphy retired from serious chess in his early twenties.
Wilhelm Steinitz became the first official world champion in 1886, beating Johannes Zukertort.
Emanuel Lasker held the world title for 27 years, from 1894 to 1921. Nobody has held it longer.
Emanuel Lasker was also a mathematician. A theorem in algebra carries his name.
José Raúl Capablanca went eight years without losing a game, from 1916 to 1924.
Alexander Alekhine is the only world champion to die while holding the title.
Max Euwe, world champion from 1935 to 1937, was a mathematics teacher.
Mikhail Botvinnik was an electrical engineer as well as a world champion.
Mikhail Tal became world champion in 1960 at the age of 23.
Bobby Fischer won the world title in Reykjavik in 1972.
Bobby Fischer won the 1963-64 US Championship with a perfect score: 11 wins from 11 games.
Bobby Fischer won 20 games in a row against top opposition in 1970 and 1971.
Bobby Fischer became a grandmaster at 15, in 1958.
Anatoly Karpov became world champion in 1975 without playing a match, when Fischer did not defend.
Anatoly Karpov was world champion from 1975 to 1985.
The first Karpov-Kasparov match was stopped after 48 games with no winner declared.
Garry Kasparov became world champion in 1985 at the age of 22.
Garry Kasparov's peak rating of 2851 stood as the record until Magnus Carlsen passed it in 2013.
Vladimir Kramnik took the title from Kasparov in 2000 without losing a single game of the match.
Viswanathan Anand became India's first grandmaster in 1988.
Magnus Carlsen became a grandmaster at 13.
Magnus Carlsen held the world title from 2013 to 2023.
Magnus Carlsen went 125 classical games without a loss, from 2018 to 2020.
Magnus Carlsen's peak classical rating of 2882 is the highest ever recorded.
Ding Liren became world champion in 2023.
Gukesh Dommaraju became the youngest world champion in history in 2024, at 18.
Vera Menchik became the first women's world champion in 1927.
Nona Gaprindashvili became the first woman awarded the grandmaster title, in 1978.
Judit Polgár became a grandmaster at 15, breaking Bobby Fischer's record.
Judit Polgár reached number eight in the world, the highest any woman has ever been ranked.
Hou Yifan became women's world champion at 16.
Abhimanyu Mishra became the youngest grandmaster in history in 2021, at 12 years and 4 months.
Sergey Karjakin held the record for youngest grandmaster for 19 years.
FIDE, the world chess federation, was founded in Paris in 1924.
FIDE's motto is "Gens una sumus": we are one family.
FIDE first awarded the grandmaster title in 1950.
The first official Chess Olympiad was held in London in 1927.
International Chess Day is July 20, the date FIDE was founded.
The Elo rating system is named after Arpad Elo, a physics professor.
US Chess adopted the Elo system in 1960. FIDE adopted it in 1970.
Elo is a surname, not an acronym, so it is not written in capitals.
The US Chess Federation was founded in 1939.
The Mechanics' Institute in San Francisco has hosted a chess club since 1854.
The Marshall Chess Club in New York was founded in 1915.
The Zürich chess society, founded in 1809, is the oldest chess club in the world.
Mechanical chess clocks were first used at the London tournament of 1883.
The Lewis chessmen were carved in the 12th century, mostly from walrus ivory.
The Lewis chessmen were found on a Scottish island in 1831.
The Turk, a chess-playing "machine" built in 1770, was a hoax. A strong player was hidden inside.
Benjamin Franklin wrote an essay called "The Morals of Chess".
William Caxton's book on chess was one of the first books printed in English.
Philidor, the best player of the 1700s, was also a successful opera composer.
Philidor wrote that pawns are the soul of chess.
In 1970 the cosmonauts of Soyuz 9 played a game against ground control. It was a draw.
In 2008 an astronaut on the International Space Station played a game against the public on Earth.
Chessboxing is a real sport. Its first event was held in 2003.
The longest world championship game is game 6 of Carlsen against Nepomniachtchi in 2021. It ran 136 moves.
The longest tournament game on record is Nikolić against Arsović, Belgrade 1989: 269 moves, drawn.
Armenia teaches chess as a compulsory subject in primary school.
The International Olympic Committee recognises chess as a sport.
The World Chess Hall of Fame is in St. Louis, Missouri.
In 1999 Garry Kasparov played a game against "the World", with the public voting on each move. He won in 62 moves.
Timur Gareyev played 48 games at once, blindfolded, in 2016.
The 1972 world championship match in Reykjavik made front pages around the world.

# --- openings
The Scotch Game is named after a correspondence match between Edinburgh and London in the 1820s.
The French Defence is named after a correspondence match between London and Paris in 1834.
The English Opening is named after Howard Staunton, who played it in 1843.
The Caro-Kann is named after two players, Horatio Caro and Marcus Kann.
The Grünfeld Defence is named after Ernst Grünfeld, who introduced it in 1922.
The Najdorf Variation is named after Miguel Najdorf.
The Benko Gambit is named after Pal Benko.
Alekhine's Defence, 1.e4 Nf6, invites White's pawns forward on purpose.
The Orangutan, 1.b4, got its name after Savielly Tartakower visited a zoo in 1924.
The Dragon Variation of the Sicilian is named after the constellation Draco.
The Ruy López is also called the Spanish Game.
The Italian Game is one of the oldest recorded openings.
Frank Marshall unveiled the Marshall Attack against Capablanca in 1918. Capablanca won anyway.
Kramnik's Berlin Defence frustrated Kasparov so much in 2000 that it became known as the Berlin Wall.
The Queen's Gambit is not a true gambit. White can usually win the pawn back.
The Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings sorts every opening into 500 codes, from A00 to E99.
The Sicilian Defence is the most popular reply to 1.e4 at master level.
The Bongcloud is 1.e4 e5 2.Ke2. Carlsen and Nakamura once played it against each other and drew by repetition.
The King's Gambit, 1.e4 e5 2.f4, was the height of fashion in the 1800s.
The London System takes its name from the London tournament of 1922.
The Fried Liver Attack is a real opening name. In Italian it is the Fegatello.
The Hippopotamus is a real opening setup. So is the Hedgehog.
The Grob, 1.g4, is named after the Swiss player Henri Grob.
The Réti Opening is named after Richard Réti, who beat Capablanca with it in 1924.
The Nimzo-Indian Defence is named after Aron Nimzowitsch.
Petrov's Defence is also called the Russian Game.
The Catalan Opening got its name from a tournament in Barcelona in 1929.
The Dutch Defence begins 1.d4 f5.
The Scandinavian Defence, 1.e4 d5, is one of the oldest recorded openings.
The Sicilian Defence begins 1.e4 c5.
The Caro-Kann begins 1.e4 c6.
The French Defence begins 1.e4 e6.
The Ruy López begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5.
The Italian Game begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4.
The Queen's Gambit begins 1.d4 d5 2.c4.
The King's Indian Defence lets White build a big centre and then attacks it.
The Pirc Defence is named after the Slovenian grandmaster Vasja Pirc.
The Evans Gambit is named after a Welsh sea captain, William Davies Evans.
The Budapest Gambit begins 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e5.
The Vienna Game begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3.
The Englund Gambit begins 1.d4 e5.
The Smith-Morra Gambit is White's pawn sacrifice against the Sicilian: 1.e4 c5 2.d4.
The Poisoned Pawn Variation is named for a pawn on b2 that is dangerous to take.

# --- words
Zugzwang is German for "compulsion to move". It is when every available move makes your position worse.
Zwischenzug is German for "in-between move".
Fianchetto is Italian for "little flank".
"Gambit" comes from an Italian word for tripping someone up.
"En prise" describes a piece left where it can simply be taken.
"Luft" is German for air. In chess it means giving your king an escape square.
"Patzer", a weak player, comes from German.
A "kibitzer" is someone who comments on a game they are not playing. The word comes from Yiddish.
A "tabiya" is a standard opening position where the real game begins.
Nimzowitsch made the word "prophylaxis" famous in chess: stopping the opponent's idea before it starts.
An "isolani" is an isolated queen's pawn.
A "desperado" is a doomed piece that captures as much as it can before it goes.
A "windmill" is a repeating series of discovered checks.
In chess, "the exchange" means a rook traded for a bishop or a knight.
A "fortress" is a position the stronger side cannot break down.
"Opposition" is when the two kings face each other with one square between them.
"Triangulation" is losing a move on purpose with your king.
A "battery" is two pieces lined up on the same file, rank or diagonal.
A "skewer" is a pin in reverse: the more valuable piece stands in front.
An "absolute pin" is a pin against the king. The pinned piece cannot legally move.
A "smothered mate" is delivered by a knight against a king boxed in by its own pieces.
The "Greek gift" is the bishop sacrifice on h7.
"Légal's mate" is named after an 18th-century French player.
"Boden's mate" is delivered by two bishops on crossing diagonals.
"Anastasia's mate" takes its name from a novel published in 1803.
The "Arabian mate", with rook and knight, is one of the oldest recorded mating patterns.
The "Lucena position" is the key winning method in rook endings.
The "Philidor position" is the key drawing method in rook endings.
"The square of the pawn" tells you whether a king can catch a passed pawn without calculating.
A "passed pawn" has no enemy pawns in front of it or on the files beside it.
A "backward pawn" cannot be protected by another pawn and cannot safely advance.
A "tempo" is one move's worth of time.
A "novelty" is a move nobody has played before in that position.
Time trouble has a German name that players use everywhere: "Zeitnot".
"Minor pieces" are bishops and knights. "Major pieces" are rooks and queens.
The Saavedra position of 1895 is won by promoting to a rook, not a queen.
The Lasker Trap ends with a pawn promoting to a knight on move seven.
The Noah's Ark Trap wins a bishop in the Ruy López.
The Elephant Trap is a trap in the Queen's Gambit Declined.
A "simul" is one player taking on many boards at the same time.
"Blindfold chess" is played without sight of the board. Moves are called out loud.
"Correspondence chess" is played by post or server, with days for each move.
"Fingerfehler" is German for a slip of the finger: playing a move you did not mean.
The "initiative" belongs to the side making threats that have to be answered.
"Overloading" is giving one defender more jobs than it can do.
A "discovered check" comes from the piece that was uncovered, not the piece that moved.

# --- machines
Alan Turing wrote a chess program before any computer existed that could run it. He worked through its moves by hand.
Claude Shannon published the first paper on programming a computer to play chess in 1950.
Deep Blue beat Garry Kasparov in a match in 1997, by 3.5 to 2.5.
Deep Blue won its first game against Kasparov in 1996, but lost that match.
Deep Blue searched about 200 million positions per second.
In 2017 AlphaZero taught itself chess in a matter of hours, knowing only the rules.
Stockfish is free and open source. Anyone can read its code.
Leela Chess Zero is an open-source engine that learned chess by playing against itself.
Modern engines are rated above 3500. The best human in history peaked at 2882.
Stockfish added a neural network to its evaluation in 2020.
In 1988 Deep Thought became the first computer to beat a grandmaster in a regular tournament game.
Belle was the first chess machine to earn a master rating, in 1983.
Mac Hack VI was the first program to play in a human tournament, in 1967.
Lichess is free, carries no ads and is open source. It launched in 2010.
Chess.com launched in 2007.
The Internet Chess Server went online in 1992.
The Glicko rating system was invented by Mark Glickman in 1995.
An engine evaluation of +1.00 means an advantage worth roughly one pawn.
A "centipawn" is a real unit. It is one hundredth of a pawn.
An endgame tablebase does not calculate. It looks the answer up.
The first complete seven-piece tablebases took up about 140 terabytes.
The "horizon effect" is when an engine pushes a problem just past the depth it can see.
The minimax idea behind chess engines goes back to John von Neumann in 1928.
Alpha-beta pruning lets an engine skip moves it can prove do not matter.
In 2005 two amateurs with ordinary computers won a "freestyle" tournament ahead of grandmasters with engines.

# --- ratings and titles
A 400-point rating gap means the stronger player is expected to score about 91 percent.
A 200-point rating gap means the stronger player is expected to score about 76 percent.
Two players with equal ratings each have an expected score of exactly 50 percent.
The grandmaster title requires a 2500 rating and three norms.
The International Master title requires a 2400 rating. FIDE Master requires 2300.
A chess title is for life. A rating is not.
In US Chess, 2200 is National Master, 2000 is Expert and 1800 is Class A.
A "norm" is a tournament performance at title level.
A "performance rating" is the rating your results in one event would suggest.
The K-factor decides how quickly a rating moves. New players get a higher one.
A draw against a higher-rated player gains you rating points.
The Elo system has no maximum rating.
Fabiano Caruana's peak rating of 2844 is the third highest in history.
Fabiano Caruana began the 2014 Sinquefield Cup with seven straight wins.
Hikaru Nakamura has won the US Championship five times.
In 1972 Bobby Fischer was rated 125 points above the world number two.
