# --- the world champions, in order
Wilhelm Steinitz was born in Prague in 1836.
Steinitz held the world title until 1894, when Emanuel Lasker took it from him.
Steinitz lost a rematch to Lasker in Moscow in 1896-97.
Emanuel Lasker was 25 when he became world champion.
Lasker lost the title to Capablanca in Havana in 1921 without winning a single game.
Emanuel Lasker earned a doctorate in mathematics in 1900.
Albert Einstein wrote the foreword to a biography of Emanuel Lasker. They were friends.
José Raúl Capablanca was born in Havana in 1888.
The story goes that Capablanca learned chess at four, by watching his father play.
Capablanca worked as a diplomat for Cuba while he was world champion.
Capablanca lost the title to Alexander Alekhine in Buenos Aires in 1927.
The 1927 Capablanca-Alekhine match lasted 34 games, and 25 of them were draws.
Capablanca and Alekhine never played a rematch.
Alekhine lost the title to Max Euwe in 1935 and took it back in 1937.
Alexander Alekhine died in Estoril, Portugal, in 1946, still world champion.
After Alekhine's death, FIDE held a five-player match tournament in 1948 to crown a new champion. Mikhail Botvinnik won it.
Max Euwe was president of FIDE from 1970 to 1978.
Mikhail Botvinnik lost the title twice and won it back twice, in rematches against Smyslov and Tal.
Vasily Smyslov was world champion from 1957 to 1958.
Vasily Smyslov trained as an opera baritone and auditioned for the Bolshoi Theatre in 1950.
Vasily Smyslov reached the Candidates final in 1984, in his sixties.
Mikhail Tal held the world title for one year, from 1960 to 1961.
Mikhail Tal was born in Riga, Latvia. His nickname was "the Magician from Riga".
Tigran Petrosian took the title from Botvinnik in 1963.
Tigran Petrosian was nicknamed "Iron Tigran" for how hard he was to beat.
Petrosian held the world title until 1969, when Boris Spassky beat him.
Boris Spassky was world champion from 1969 to 1972.
Bobby Fischer lost the first game of the 1972 match and forfeited the second, then won the title anyway.
Bobby Fischer was the first American-born world champion.
On his way to the 1972 title, Fischer beat Mark Taimanov and Bent Larsen 6-0 each in Candidates matches.
Bobby Fischer won the US Championship all eight times he played in it.
Bobby Fischer became US champion at 14.
Fischer and Spassky played a rematch in Yugoslavia in 1992, twenty years after Reykjavik.
Bobby Fischer spent his last years in Iceland and is buried there.
Bobby Fischer died in 2008 at the age of 64, one year for every square on the board.
Garry Kasparov and Anatoly Karpov played five world championship matches between 1984 and 1990.
Kasparov and Karpov played 144 world championship games against each other.
Garry Kasparov was born Garik Weinstein, in Baku.
Kasparov split from FIDE in 1993 to play Nigel Short for a title of his own.
From 1993 to 2006 there were two world champions: FIDE's, and the "classical" line from Steinitz.
Vladimir Kramnik reunified the world title in 2006 by beating Veselin Topalov.
The 2006 Kramnik-Topalov match had a forfeited game over a dispute about bathroom breaks.
Viswanathan Anand won the world title in 2007, in a tournament in Mexico City.
Anand defended his title against Kramnik in 2008, Topalov in 2010 and Gelfand in 2012.
Anand lost the world title to Magnus Carlsen in 2013, in Chennai, his home city.
Viswanathan Anand was nicknamed "the Lightning Kid" for how fast he played.
Magnus Carlsen defended his title against Anand, Karjakin, Caruana and Nepomniachtchi.
All twelve classical games of the 2018 Carlsen-Caruana match were drawn. Rapid tiebreaks decided it.
Magnus Carlsen chose not to defend his world title in 2023.
Magnus Carlsen became world number one in January 2010, at 19.
Ding Liren beat Ian Nepomniachtchi in rapid tiebreaks in 2023 to become world champion.
Ding Liren is the first player from China to hold the open world title.
Gukesh Dommaraju beat Ding Liren 7.5-6.5 in Singapore in 2024.
Gukesh won the 2024 Candidates tournament at 17, the youngest player ever to do it.
Gukesh became a grandmaster at 12 years and 7 months.
Botvinnik ran a chess school whose pupils included Karpov, Kasparov and Kramnik.
Anatoly Karpov is credited with more than 160 tournament victories.
Garry Kasparov retired from professional chess in 2005.
Garry Kasparov was world number one for most of the 21 years from 1984 to 2005.
The Candidates tournament decides who gets to challenge the world champion.

# --- women's world champions and pioneers
Vera Menchik held the women's world title from 1927 until her death in 1944.
Male masters who lost to Vera Menchik were jokingly enrolled in the "Menchik Club".
Lyudmila Rudenko became the second women's world champion in 1950.
Nona Gaprindashvili held the women's world title from 1962 to 1978.
Maia Chiburdanidze became women's world champion in 1978, at 17.
Maia Chiburdanidze and Nona Gaprindashvili are both from Georgia, which dominated women's chess for decades.
Susan Polgár won the women's world championship in 1996.
László Polgár taught his three daughters chess at home to prove that geniuses are made, not born.
Sofia Polgár scored 8.5 out of 9 at a tournament in Rome in 1989, at the age of 14.
Judit Polgár beat Garry Kasparov in a rapid game in 2002.
Judit Polgár never played in the women's world championship. She played in the open events instead.
Hou Yifan won the women's world championship four times.
Ju Wenjun first became women's world champion in 2018.
The first Women's Chess Olympiad was held in 1957.

# --- prodigies and records
Rameshbabu Praggnanandhaa became a grandmaster at 12 years and 10 months.
Hikaru Nakamura became a grandmaster at 15, breaking Bobby Fischer's American record.
Alireza Firouzja became the youngest player ever to pass a 2800 rating, at 18.
Abhimanyu Mishra, the youngest grandmaster ever, is from New Jersey.
Paul Morphy played eight blindfold games at once in Paris in 1858.
Paul Morphy was from New Orleans.
Paul Morphy finished law school before he was old enough to practise law.
Paul Morphy offered pawn-and-move odds to any player in the world. Nobody took him up on it.
George Koltanowski played 34 blindfold games at once in Edinburgh in 1937.
Miguel Najdorf played 45 blindfold games at once in São Paulo in 1947.
Miguel Najdorf was in Argentina for the 1939 Olympiad when the Second World War began, and he stayed for good.
In 2011 Ehsan Ghaem Maghami played 604 opponents at once, a world record at the time.
Adolf Anderssen, winner of London 1851, was a mathematics teacher.
Steinitz is the one who named Anderssen's 1852 win "the Evergreen Game".
Hikaru Nakamura is one of the most-watched chess streamers in the world.
Magnus Carlsen has won the World Rapid and World Blitz championships many times between them.

# --- older history
Chaturanga's four kinds of pieces stood for the four divisions of an Indian army: infantry, cavalry, elephants and chariots.
In shatranj the queen's ancestor, the fers, could only step one square diagonally.
In shatranj the bishop's ancestor, the alfil, jumped exactly two squares diagonally.
Chess reached Europe through Moorish Spain and Sicily, by around the 10th century.
Some medieval players rolled dice to decide which piece to move.
The pawn's two-square first move became standard in Europe at about the same time as the powerful new queen.
Castling rules differed from country to country until the 1800s.
For a time in England, stalemating your opponent counted as a loss for the player who did it.
In shatranj, stalemating your opponent counted as a win.
Medieval Europeans called chess a game for kings, and it was popular at royal courts.
The English word "chess" comes from the Old French "esches", the plural of "eschec", meaning check.
The English treasury is called the Exchequer after a chequered cloth used for counting money, like a chessboard.
Howard Staunton beat Pierre Saint-Amant in a match in Paris in 1843, regarded as an unofficial world championship.
Howard Staunton also edited a complete edition of Shakespeare.
The Café de la Régence in Paris was Europe's chess capital for more than a century. Philidor and Morphy both played there.
Philidor gave blindfold exhibitions in London in the 1780s, and newspapers treated them as a marvel.
The first newspaper chess column appeared in the Liverpool Mercury in 1813.
The Edinburgh-London correspondence match of the 1820s took four years to finish.
The codebreakers at Bletchley Park included the chess masters Hugh Alexander, Stuart Milner-Barry and Harry Golombek.
The 1939 Chess Olympiad in Buenos Aires was still being played when the Second World War broke out.
The Soviet Union won 18 of the 19 Chess Olympiads it entered from 1952 to 1990.
Hungary won the 1978 Olympiad, the only one the Soviet Union lost between 1952 and 1990.
The United States won four Chess Olympiads in a row in the 1930s.
The United States won the Chess Olympiad in 2016.
India won both the open and the women's gold medals at the 2024 Olympiad in Budapest.
Iran banned chess after its 1979 revolution and lifted the ban in 1988.
Napoleon loved chess. By most accounts he was not very good at it.
The Mechanical Turk is said to have played both Napoleon and Benjamin Franklin.
El Ajedrecista, built in Spain in 1912 by Leonardo Torres Quevedo, could play king and rook against king on its own.
Arabic players were composing chess problems, called mansubat, more than a thousand years ago.
The Swiss system was first used at a tournament in Zürich in 1895.
The Hastings Chess Congress has been held over Christmas and New Year almost every year since 1920.
The Tata Steel tournament in Wijk aan Zee has been held since 1938. Players call it the Wimbledon of chess.
The Saint Louis Chess Club opened in 2008. Rex Sinquefield founded it.
The Sinquefield Cup in St. Louis was first held in 2013.
Chess was a medal event at the Asian Games in 2006, 2010 and 2022.
The 1972 Fischer-Spassky match was played in Reykjavik's Laugardalshöll sports hall.

# --- famous games and moments
The "Game of the Century" was played by a 13-year-old Bobby Fischer against Donald Byrne in 1956.
In the Game of the Century, Fischer gave up his queen on move 17 and won.
Kasparov's win over Veselin Topalov at Wijk aan Zee in 1999 is often called Kasparov's Immortal.
Legend says spectators showered the board with gold coins after Frank Marshall's queen move Qg3 in 1912.
Marshall's Qg3 against Levitsky in 1912 left his queen attackable three ways, and every capture lost.
Game 6 of the 1972 match was so good that Spassky applauded it along with the audience.
Deep Blue's win in game 1 of 1996 was the first time a computer beat a reigning world champion in a game with classical time controls.
In game 2 of their 1997 match, Kasparov resigned against Deep Blue in a position that was later shown to be a draw.
Carlsen and Nepomniachtchi's 136-move game in 2021 lasted almost eight hours.
The "Peruvian Immortal" was played in 1934 by Esteban Canal, who gave up his queen and both rooks.
Rotlewi against Rubinstein, Łódź 1907, is famous as "Rubinstein's Immortal".
Paul Morphy's Opera Game ended in mate on move 17.
In 1834 Louis de la Bourdonnais and Alexander McDonnell played 85 games against each other in London.
Fischer's 20-game winning streak in 1970 and 1971 included two 6-0 match wins over top grandmasters.

# --- the Elo and titles
Arpad Elo was born in Hungary and moved to the United States as a child.
The Woman Grandmaster title, WGM, was introduced in 1976.
FIDE awarded its first 27 grandmaster titles in 1950.
The Candidate Master title, CM, requires a FIDE rating of 2200.
FIDE titles are awarded at the FIDE Congress, usually once or twice a year.
A FIDE rating starts at 1400 at the bottom of the scale.
US Chess ratings and FIDE ratings are separate. The same player usually has two different numbers.
US Chess ratings start at 100. Nobody can go lower.
In US Chess, Class B is 1600 to 1799, Class C is 1400 to 1599 and Class D is 1200 to 1399.
In US Chess, a Senior Master is rated 2400 or higher.
The Glicko system adds a "ratings deviation" that says how sure the system is about your number.
A provisional rating moves faster because the system does not know you yet.
